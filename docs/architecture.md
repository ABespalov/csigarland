# csiGarland Software Architecture

What the firmware in `firmware/app` does now. Plans are in [`stubs/ROADMAP.md`](stubs/ROADMAP.md), and how to build and use it is in the [guides](guides/README.md).

## What It Does

* **Startup:** the first thing the application does, on a cold start and after any reset, is open the rail for a moment, send two black frames for 256 LEDs and close it again. LEDs on an ungated rail keep their last colours until then, through the bootloader. It then stays in standby with the rail off and checks for a garland.
* **POWER (GPIO 3):** a click in standby turns on at the remembered brightness (50 % at first); when on, a click steps brightness 10 → 25 → 50 → 75 → 100 → 10 %; a long press (0.8 s) starts a 5 s countdown on the screen, with the garland still lit, and then turns off.
* **EFFECT (GPIO 20):** when on, a click moves to the next favourite effect and a double click goes to the first favourite. With a playlist set, a long press starts it, or stops it and keeps what plays; with none it does nothing. The button component tells a double click from a click, and sends a click only once no second press has come. See the [Effects Guide](guides/effects.md).
* **SPEED (GPIO 23):** when on, a click steps speed 0.25x → 0.5x → 0.75x → 1x → 1.5x → 2x → 3x → 0.25x and a long press returns to 1x. From a speed set on the portal between these, a click goes to the next step up, or to 0.25x from 3x or more.
* **Any button** cancels the countdown or wakes the screen from a screensaver. That press does nothing else.
* **Display:** LVGL screen in csiPass's design and palette: pixel wordmark (`csi` white, `Garland` blue), build line and rule, a framed body with a status icon, status word, effect name and one evenly spaced row of brightness, speed and LED count, each a glyph in the frame's colour and a white value (LED count: plain `N` when set manually, `~N` when estimated, `0` when none are detected, `?` when present but not measurable), and a framed footer with the portal address. The whole screen turns amber when no LEDs are detected. The image is rotated 180 degrees from the panel's default, with its top at the board's USB end.
* **Screensaver:** ported from csiPass: None, Starfield (default), Flying Clock (local time, or uptime before the first time sync), Breathing Dot, Black Screen (backlight off), and Scheduler24, which flies as the Flying Clock does: the soonest of what comes, the schedule's next step that does something within a day (`schedule_next_step`, while the clock, the schedule and the zone are known) or the playlist's next effect while it plays, lit, at the moment the Lighting task publishes in one word (`playlist_next_ms`), as `next: Fireplace` and `in 2h 05m`; with nothing to come, the latest of what came since the start, on the uptime clock: the start itself, a turn on or off, which the display sees in the state at every poll, the LEDs' rail on or off apart from that, a rail that follows a turn on or off within 5 s being part of it, and the schedule's last step done that did something, told from the playlist the step was decided on under its lock, not one filled or emptied since (`apply_scheduled`), named as it was then and timed in 64 bits, as it may stay the last for weeks and its effect's place be another's by then (`schedule_last_step`), as `last: Off` and `3h 10m ago`. A step is named by the effect it plays, the playlist, or what it does to the power, the brightness or the speed, in that order; the words are worked out each second, to the second within the hour (`src/saver_schedule.h`). They take at most 80 % of the screen's width, wrap to more lines up to 80 % of its height, and past that take a smaller font, 28, 20, 16 and 14 in turn (`src/saver_view.cpp`, which draws every screensaver, here and for the documents' pictures). It is offered only with the clock module, which keeps the time through a power cut; one chosen before the module was taken out runs as the Flying Clock. It starts when no button has been pressed for the delay, 10-600 s with a default of 60. The portal sets both, and it never runs over the countdown, an update, an access request or the making of keys.
* **Screen brightness:** the backlight is dimmed by PWM. The portal sets its level for normal use (default 100 %) and during a screensaver (default 30 %), each 5-100 %, applied at once and saved. Black Screen always turns it off.
* **LED brightness:** the percent is perceptual (gamma 2.2), so 10, 25, 50 and 75 % send about 0.5, 5, 22 and 53 % of the light. Brightness and the limiter work in 1/256ths of an LED step, and temporal dithering carries that to the LEDs: frames go out every 2.5 ms, 400 a second, each channel alternating between the two steps around its level. A chain too long for that dithers as fast as it can be sent (about 120 frames a second at 256 LEDs). Effects are still drawn about 60 times a second, and at once when the chain on the wire changes length, so that no frame goes out over the new length before the whole of it was drawn and held to the current limit.
* **Current limit (`src/power_model.h`):** no sensor measures the rail, so each frame's current is worked out from its colours: an LED at full white draws the owner's white figure above its idle draw, each channel a third of it, linear in the channel's level, and every LED its idle draw dark. Measured on one WS2812B of the owner's garland: 36.1 mA above 0.51 mA idle, the three channels within 3 % of a third each and their sum within 0.3 % of white, and half a level drawing half. A frame over the limit is scaled down whole, its lit part to the budget past the idle draw. The limit is worked out from the Device page's settings, kept in NVS: what the supply (500-20000 mA, default 2000) gives beyond 250 mA for the board, the screen and Wi-Fi, which share it, less the margin, a share of that (5-20 %, default 20), so (2000 - 250) * 80 % = 1400 mA by default, as in the releases before these settings; an LED at full white (5-100 mA, default 40, the measurement and room for a batch's spread) and dark (0-5 mA, default 1). The four share one word of the state, so the Lighting task takes a save whole, at its next frame, and one record of NVS, `power_v1`, the same word, which a write leaves whole, the old or the new: four keys cut short between them could leave a mix that allows more than either. A record that holds no valid settings is taken for none. Settings that cannot be read at a start are read again every 5 s with the LED count, and are not saved over meanwhile, as the screen's are not; until they are, or are found to be none, which takes the defaults, the rail stays off, as the defaults may allow more current than what was saved. The word holds whether they are known beside them. The rail also stays off, or goes off at once, while the chain's idle draw alone, its length in use by the idle figure, is over the limit, which no dimming lowers: a turn on asked for stays asked for, and comes once the settings, or the count, allow it (`rail_may_be_on`); the Device page and the utility say so in place of dimming. A count the probe found may be that of a chain since replaced, which a presence check does not count again: held off by it, with the count automatic and the settings known, the chain is counted again, the rail off, once for each turn on asked for, and the turn that reads the new count decides.
* **Effects:** described as data, with a catalog of ready and planned (`todo`) effects. See the [Effects Guide](guides/effects.md). They ship as a pack beside the firmware, in two 96 KB flash slots written in turn (`src/effect_pack_store.cpp`), so a flash, an update file or an effects-only update can replace them. The owner can add up to 32 from files, kept in the 64 KB `effects` flash partition (`src/effect_store.cpp`).
* **Screens:** after csiPass, `src/screen_model.h` holds one row per screen: its colour, title, text, and whether a screensaver may cover it. One function chooses the screen from the state, in the order update, making keys, access request, power-off countdown, setup network, no LEDs, playing, standby; `display_view.cpp` only draws the result, and knows nothing of the panel, which `display.cpp` drives. So `tools/screens` can draw the same screens to files for the documents, and fails if a screen has no picture. A new screen or a change of colour is a change to `screen_model.h`.
* **Screen colours:** the accent colours the rule, frames, icon, title, stat glyphs and progress bar, and says one thing: grey, nothing is happening (standby); blue, running and all is well (playing); amber, the owner has something to do or must not interfere (no LEDs, setup network, power-off countdown, update under way, access request, making keys); green, an operation has finished well (update installed). Red is kept for a fault the board cannot get over, and no screen uses it yet. Whatever the state, text and the bottom line are white, the version and the clock module's glyph grey, and the wordmark its own colours.
* **Web portal:** six pages, and one for pairing a browser, sharing one stylesheet and script, each under a header with the firmware version: Effects (`/`) shows the current mode, plays any ready effect and picks favourites, Schedule (`/schedule`) holds the next action, the actions, the time zone and the clock module, Device (`/device`) the LED count (automatic or 1-256), the current limit's settings, with the limit, the chain's draw at full white and how many LEDs that leaves undimmed worked out as they are typed, and the screen, Network (`/network`) the home network, the mDNS name, the root certificate with how to install it, and the owner's domain, Firmware (`/firmware`) updates, Info (`/info`) the board, memory, addresses, mode, next action and paired browsers. Favourites, the LED count, the name, the domain and Wi-Fi settings are saved in NVS. On the setup network a captive portal opens the Network page. `scripts/portal_pack.py` gzips all of this at build time, to about a third of its size as the build prints it, and flash holds only that form. The stylesheet and script are served under names holding a hash of their content (`/s.<hash>.css`), which browsers may keep for good; the pages link to the current names and are checked with the garland on each load. Class names are one letter, and the pages carry no inline styles. A blue button is the one main action of its block, grey is any other, equals with no main one among them included, and red text marks one that loses something (Delete, Forget). In a list, the blue button is what is in force (Active).
* **Network:** with no home network configured, or after 20 s without it, the access point `csiGarland-Setup` (WPA2, with the board's own password on its screen) runs at `http://192.168.4.1`, with a captive portal that answers every DNS name with that address. On the home network the portal is at `https://` and the address shown on the display, or `https://csigarland.local` (mDNS, under a name the Network page can change, which restarts the garland, as the certificate holds the name), and an access point that was running stops 60 s after connecting. The station picks the strongest access point of the network's name, tries it 3 times before moving to the next, and runs without Wi-Fi power save, as the unit is mains powered.
* **Time:** the clock syncs with `pool.ntp.org` on every home network connection and hourly after that. Without a clock module, after a power cut it stays unset until the next sync. The first browser to open the portal sets the time zone from its own, while `zone_set` says none was chosen; the portal can change it. A saved zone that cannot be read at a start, after three tries, is no zone missing: the clock goes by UTC meanwhile, `zone_set` stays true so that no browser sets its own over it, the schedule does no step, as its steps are in local time, and waits as for a schedule not read yet, and the scheduler's task reads it again every 5 s; a zone the owner sets meanwhile is newer, and stands.
* **Clock module (optional):** a DS3231 on the `TX` and `RX` pads, wiring in [`hardware/control-unit.md`](hardware/control-unit.md). Looked for at startup; without one everything works as above. A module that has kept its time sets the clock at once, so the schedule works with no network, and it follows NTP hourly when it has drifted by 2 s or more. One that lost its time (the part's oscillator-stopped flag, or a date before 2026) is not believed: the first NTP answer sets it. The portal's Schedule section shows it and sets it from the browser's clock, from NTP or by hand, and the next NTP answer overrules a time set there. Since these pads are UART0, logs go over USB.
* **Fades (`src/light_fade.h`):** the Lighting task brings the light up over 0.8 s at a turn on, and down over 0.8 s before the power sequence of a turn off, which waits for it: the rail stays on and the effect drawn meanwhile. The fade is a level, linear in time and squared into light, by which the owner's brightness gain is scaled, so the dim end goes evenly; a turn on during a fade out goes back up from where it got to. A scheduled turn on with a rise takes 60 s instead, and so does only that one: the `rise` bit of the play word is set by a scheduled step alone and cleared by any other order.
* **Playlist and cross-fades:** up to 16 effects by catalog position, each played for 10 s to an hour, saved in NVS by id (`src/playlist_logic.h`). The Lighting task moves it on, by a compare-and-swap that only takes if the playlist still plays from the effect it was worked out from, and from the same start, so a choice or a start made meanwhile stands: a start counts in two bits of the same word, so two or three starts between two looks of the task still show, and the task times the effect afresh after an update held it up, however many starts came meanwhile; any choice of one effect, by a button, a page or a scheduled step, clears the `playlist` bit in the same word. A scheduled step can start it instead, held in the entry's effect field as `@playlist`, which no effect's id can be, so the layout 1.0.0 saved stays; it turns the garland on only if the playlist has effects, which the state decides on the one reading of the list it starts from, between what the step comes to with the playlist and what it comes to without; what changes the list and what starts it from what it reads hold one lock, never the Lighting task, so a list emptied cannot cross a start. A rise is saved in `schedule_v1` as a plain turn on, and which entries rise under `schedule_rises`: two marks, each a mask, bit by place, with a hash of the `schedule_v1` bytes it holds for, one for the list saved and one for the list before it. The marks are written first: NVS writes each blob whole and joins no two, so the list a failed or cut second write leaves is the one before, which keeps its rises, and a list refused never comes into force after a restart. Once the list is saved, the marks are written a third time with the new one's alone, so no mark of the list before can match a list saved later, or a garland gone back to 1.0.0 and saved there; that third write failing leaves the two, which still read right. Two equal entries, one rising and one not, keep their own, and a list 1.0.0 saved since takes none of it; the entries whole, which builds before the marks kept there, are still read, by equality, until the schedule is saved again. A firmware gone back to 1.0.0 reads all of it as it meant there. A playlist moving on is no choice of the owner's: the effect and the moment remembered stay those of the start, so scheduled steps since still count at the next start; of a playlist and an effect in one minute the later in the list wins. Missed minutes replayed at a start are combined minute by minute, so a rise comes with the effect of its own minute, and a later minute's turn on is a plain one. The screen's title says where it stands, `Playing 3/12`, in the body's font, and `/api/state` gives `playlist_at` and `playlist_size`. At every change of effect while lit, the last frame of the old one is kept and mixed into the new one's frames over the cross-fade, 0 to 5 s and 1 s unless set with the playlist, before brightness and the current limit, which so apply to the mix as to any frame. An uploaded effect in the playlist cannot be deleted while it is there. Whenever the catalog changes, by an upload or a deletion of an effect, the state takes the saved list again, so an effect back in the catalog plays from it as the list shows it; choosing an effect, a favourite or a preview changes no catalog, and reads nothing (`src/playlist_resolver.cpp`). A reading of the list that still fails after three tries, settings that could not be opened at the start among them, leaves it stale: the scheduler's task reads it again every 5 s until it is read, and the playlist's GET and Play apply the list they have just read first, so what they answer and play is what the state holds. One lock holds a reading and its applying, so an older reading never lands after a newer list is saved. A list left playing at a start, whose reading failed, or whose being left playing could not be read, which is tried again with it, plays on once it is read, unless the owner chose something since the start, or a scheduled step has chosen what plays, an effect or the playlist, a step that gave way to the owner choosing nothing: the check is in the same word as any choice, so one that comes in between is never undone. The list is kept by id: an effect the catalog lacks, as of an effects pack updated since, stays in it, is played once it is there again, comes back with the list from GET under `missing`, and is kept by a save that sends it back, as an editor changing the dwell does; only a list that held it already can keep one. Deleting an uploaded effect is checked against the saved list by id, and refused while it cannot be read. A chain made longer between two frames draws the next frame whole first, so a cross-fade never mixes in pixels from the length before.
* **Schedule:** up to 32 actions, each with a time, days of the week and steps: turn on, turn on rising over a minute, or off, brightness 1-100 %, speed (0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2 or 3), effect. Nothing can accompany "off", and starting an effect also turns the garland on. Actions run only once the clock is set. At the first sync after boot, only what came after the last saved choice is replayed as one result, up to 24 hours: the time of that choice is saved with it, so a schedule step from before it never overrides it. Choosing what is already playing counts too, and is saved like a change, as the moment is what matters. The same holds while running: a step already due when the owner chose something, in the same minute too, is not carried out, and the next one is. A mark of the owner's having chosen shares one atomic word with what it guards, cleared before a step is worked out, so a step worked out before a choice is never applied after it. A choice within a few milliseconds of a minute's start cannot be told from one just after it, and counts as in that minute. The clock choices are timed by is set before the buttons start, so a press during a slow start, before the scheduler runs, outranks the steps before it all the same. A choice saved with no clock outranks every step up to the first time known, and is saved again as of that time, so that later steps count, with another try each minute if that write fails. Actions go by local time: when the clocks go back, one in the repeated hour runs twice, and when they go forward, one in the skipped hour does not run that day. A schedule that cannot be read at a start, after three tries, is no empty list: none of it runs, the portal answers 503 for it, its timeline and its saving, and for deleting an uploaded effect it may name, and it is read again every minute. Until it is read, nothing of it is done; once it is, the minutes are done as at a start, from the last saved choice, and a choice of the owner's made since the start, saved or still waiting to be, outranks the steps before it by the moment it was made, as a choice saved with no clock does from the first time known, which is taken at the first sync whatever waits. While the playlist is not read, the minutes stop before a step that starts it and turns the garland on or leaves it, which would come to nothing and count as done, and the rest wait for the list, the start's replay going on from that step once it is read; a minute that turns off as well is done now, as off, the start of the playlist with it lost, and a plain Off of a schedule with no playlist step still comes. The replay never reaches back more than a day, a clock set late or corrected in between too. A choice saved is dated by the moment it was made, not by its save up to 10 s later, so a step between the two still comes at the next start; one made before the clock was set as at the first time known; and a save of what a scheduled step did since that choice by the moment of the save. The start's replay, once begun, goes on from where it stopped, whatever its own steps saved meanwhile; while a step waits for the list, at the start or as time passes, a save of what the steps before it did is dated just before it, so that a start in between still has it to do. The first time known is taken by whichever needs it first, the scheduler's pass or a save, so a choice made with no clock is never dated before it. While whether the playlist was left playing is not known, a save the owner or a step did not make about what plays keeps the mark of playing there, a change of only the power, brightness or speed among them; A remembered choice that is there and could not be read at the start is read again every 5 s, and nothing is saved over it until it is: read, it is taken as at a start, in one change of the state that yields to any choice the owner made since the start, in between too. Taken, or found to be none, the start's replay is done again from it, as the one done without it knew no choice and took only the steps after the first time known, so steps missed with no power still come, and those done meanwhile, which came after it, are done again on top of it: a step of the speed alone does not keep the rest from coming back. The playlist stops with it, an effect gone from the catalog too, and resumes only as it was left, so one a step started meanwhile is that step's, done again by the replay. What is then waiting to be saved counts from the record as flash holds it, whether or not it was taken, not from the state after the reads that followed it, so a turn off made during them, or before it was read, is saved at once; at the start too, saves count from what the state held before the buttons started, so a press during a slow start is a change to save. Until a replay begins, at a start or again, a save that is no choice of the owner's, as of a playlist resumed while the schedule cannot be read, is dated just before the replay's first minute, as before a step waiting for the playlist, so neither the replay nor the next start passes its steps by. Whether the playlist was left playing is read and resumed apart from what the record puts on, so a choice of only the power, brightness or speed since the start leaves it to come back; and until it is read, it is not given the first time known as its time, as it may have one. So it does while the mark, read as playing, waits for the list to resume it, as when none of the list's effects is in the catalog, until one is back, and such a change does not stop that resume, as only a choice of what plays, by the owner or a step, does. An action naming an effect the catalog lacks, as after an update of the effects, stays in the list, done once the effect is back, comes with the list from GET under `missing`, and is kept by a save that sends it back, as an editor saving another change does; only a list that held it already can keep one. The timeline runs from the last minute the scheduler has done, or the few it catches up, to a day from the time asked, so a step of a minute begun and not done yet still shows, and one done before a clock stepped back does not. The Info page and `next` say `unread` meanwhile, not that nothing is coming. Once read, and before the portal may save one, the rise marks are rewritten to hold the list in force alone, where a save cut short, or one whose last write failed, left a mark of another list beside it (`settings_tidy_schedule_rises`). A garland gone back to 1.0.0 before a start of 1.1.0 has done that keeps both marks, and a list 1.0.0 then saves could take one of them, if its bytes are just those of the list before or the one refused.
* **Portal API:** `src/portal.cpp` holds the routes and the two servers, `src/portal_effects.cpp` the garland's state, LEDs and effects, and `src/portal_settings.cpp` Wi-Fi, the schedule, the time, the screen and the current limit's settings. `GET /api/display`, `POST /api/display` `{"saver", "delay", "screen_brightness", "saver_brightness"}` (`saver`: `none`, `starfield`, `flying-clock`, `breathing-dot`, `black-screen`, `scheduler24`, this last refused with 409 with no clock module unless it is the one chosen already; `GET` says `clock_module`), A `GET` or `DELETE` sent with a body is refused with 400 before anything else, its body unread and its connection closed, and a body a route reads has 10 s to arrive whole, pairing's among them. On both servers a request's headers have 10 s, from the session's opening, on HTTPS once its handshake is done, which the watch holds to 15 s by the server's own list of clients, as ESP-IDF does it before anything of the application's is called, or from the end of the request before it, which ESP-IDF reads before any route with no limit of its own but each read's. A task of its own, `src/portal_watch.cpp`, holds each session to its deadline, the headers', a body's or an update's, and shuts down the connection of one past it, which ends a read blocked on it, inside a TLS record too; a stream handed to the events task has none. Every route's body counts as unread until a reader has taken all of it, so a route that answers early, or reads none, as `POST /api/wifi/scan`, closes its connection rather than have the server read the rest. `GET /api/schedule`, `POST /api/schedule` `{"entries", "revision"}` (replaces the whole list, and answers 409 if `revision` is given and is not the list's own, which the page always does; both also give `next`, what is due next, and `revision`), `GET /api/schedule/timeline` (the next 24 hours as `src/schedule.h` works them out, `src/portal_playlist.cpp`), `GET /api/playlist`, `POST /api/playlist` `{"effects", "dwell", "cross_fade"}`, `POST /api/playlist/play` `{"play"}`, `GET /api/info`, `POST /api/network/name` `{"name"}`, `POST /api/network/certificate` `{"domain"}` (empty for none; restarts), `GET /csigarland-root.crt`, `POST /api/auth/request` `{"label"}`, `POST /api/auth/wait` `{"request"}`, `GET /api/auth/devices`, `POST /api/auth/forget` `{"id"}`, `GET /api/time`, `POST /api/time` `{"zone"}` (POSIX TZ string), `POST /api/time/rtc` `{"source": "ntp"}`, `{"source": "browser", "epoch"}` (a whole number within 2026-2099) or `{"source": "manual", "local": "2026-10-31T18:05"}`, `GET /api/state`, `POST /api/state` `{"brightness", "speed"}` (either or both, `speed` 0.25 to 5 in steps of 0.25, and it turns nothing on), `GET /api/effects`, `POST` and `DELETE /api/effects/file?id=`, `POST /api/effects/preview`, `GET /api/effects/store`, `GET /api/effects/planned`, `POST /api/effect` `{"id"}`, `POST /api/favorites` `{"id", "favorite"}`, `POST /api/leds` `{"count"}` (0 means automatic), `GET /api/leds/power`, `POST /api/leds/power` `{"supply_ma", "led_white_ma", "led_idle_ma", "margin_percent"}` (all four, answering what `GET` does: them, `board_ma`, `limit_ma`, the chain's `leds` and `peak_ma` at full white, `undimmed_leds`, and each one's range; 503 while they could not be read), `GET /api/wifi`, `POST /api/wifi` `{"ssid", "password"}` (an empty `ssid` forgets the network), `POST /api/wifi/scan` starts a background search and `GET /api/wifi/scan` reports `{"state", "networks"}`, with `state` one of `idle`, `running`, `done`, `failed`.
* **Push updates (`src/portal_events.cpp`):** `GET /api/events`, and `/api/v1/events` for programs, are Server-Sent Events: `event: state` with what `/api/state` answers, at once and whenever it changes, and `event: alive` with `{}` within every 15 s of each stream's own silence, an event rather than a comment so that a page's script hears it too. The server hands each such request to the `events` task (`httpd_req_async_handler_begin`), so it is free at once, and the task writes the stream from then on. A stream holds its socket and TLS session while it is open, of the four the HTTPS server has, so there are two at most, and a third is answered 503, as is one that would wait more than 100 ms for the list of streams; any open when an update begins is ended, and none is opened during one. Each stream keeps what it was let in under, the paired client and its token's hash, and is ended at the next look once that client is forgotten, replaced or out of its days; a pair given with no clock starts its days at a look, as at a request. The task copies the list under its lock and writes outside it, and each stream's socket gives up a write after 1 s, so a reader that takes nothing loses its own stream and holds up the other for no longer. The socket's own timeout is put back before the stream ends, and its connection is ended while the stream still holds it, shut down both ways, so no request after it inherits the 1 s, and the server closes its own session of it; closed by its number after the hand-back, it could be another connection by then. The utility and the Effects page end a stream that sends nothing for 45 s, three `alive` events missed, and open another, the utility asking for the state until it has one. A page or program with no stream asks every few seconds, as before; the Effects page and the utility ask for nothing while theirs is open.
* **Security:** the portal runs over HTTPS on port 443, and every `/api/` route but the two that ask for access wants a paired browser, whose cookie counts only from the portal's own origin, its scheme that of the server the request came to: `SameSite` alone would let a page on another name under the owner's domain use it, and Firefox and Safari send the HTTPS cookie with a request of a page on plain HTTP under the same name, which whoever intercepts plain HTTP could put there. The [public API](stubs/PUBLIC-API.md) under `/api/v1/` holds to the same rule with a token instead of a cookie, and its open routes are its two that ask for access and `/api/v1/about`, which says only what firmware this is. The firmware's log is open to nobody unpaired, and is kept in RAM only once a client has read it. Plain HTTP on port 80 only redirects there, except on the setup network, for `/trial-log` while on trial, and if HTTPS has failed (see **A closed unit** below).
  * **Pairing (`src/portal_auth.cpp`):** a browser asks, and it and the garland's screen show the same four digits. The owner presses POWER and SPEED together on seeing them match; any one button refuses and holds off new requests for a minute. One request waits at a time, for 60 s. The digits are what stop a stranger who asks at the moment the owner presses for their own browser. The browser receives a random 256-bit token as an `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Strict` cookie for 100 days, not extended by use, and the board keeps its SHA-256 in encrypted NVS, for up to 8 browsers. A token counts only once that is saved. Forgetting takes effect in memory at once, and says so if it could not be saved, as a restart could then bring the browser back. A list that is there and cannot be read, after three tries at the start, is no empty list: it lets no browser in, nothing is saved over it, and pairing a browser or forgetting one is refused, until a later request reads it, which then lets those paired in with no restart; forgetting them all needs nothing of it, and still works. Meanwhile a route that wants pairing answers 503 with `Retry-After`, not 401, as a program takes 401 for a token gone and lets it go: decided by the request's own look at the list, so another request reading it in a moment after cannot turn the answer into 401. Without a clock the board cannot call a token expired, and starts its days at the first time it knows. The Info page lists the browsers and forgets them one by one; POWER and SPEED held for 10 s forget them all. `deploy/ota-update.ps1` pairs the same way and keeps its token in `deploy/portal-token.local.txt`. An accepted limit: the digits stop a second asker, not someone relaying. Whoever intercepts plain HTTP before its redirect to HTTPS can ask in the owner's place and show the owner the same digits, and an approval then gives them the token, as it is not tied to the owner's connection. What stops that is opening `https://` with the root installed. HSTS would do that for the browser, and is not sent on purpose: it would keep the browser from the plain HTTP that a firmware whose HTTPS has failed depends on.
  * **Certificates (`src/portal_tls.cpp`, `src/tls_logic.h`):** see [HTTPS certificates](#https-certificates).
  * **Setup network exception:** only while the setup network is up, and only while the home network's own address is outside its /24, requests to `192.168.4.1` need neither TLS nor pairing, as the phone windows that open it refuse a certificate they do not know and keep no cookies. Joining that network took the password on the screen. As no cookie stands in the way, the API, the two routes that ask for access and the other open ones among it, answers only to the portal's own names, `192.168.4.1` and `<name>.local`, and refuses a request whose `Origin` differs from the name it was sent to, under `http://`. So another page open on that phone cannot send to the portal, even under a name that network's DNS resolves to it. A page asked for under any other name is redirected to the portal's address. A request with no `Origin` is let through, as tools send none and joining took the password.
  * **Memory:** TLS uses dynamic buffers (16 KB in, 4 KB out, only while a record is in flight), P-256 only and no session cache or renegotiation, at most 4 sessions. Measured on a board during development: 106 KB lowest free heap with TLS working, under light use. An upload over TLS with other pages open has not been measured.
  * **Setup network:** each board makes its own random 12-character password once and shows it on the screen only while the access point runs, so the Wi-Fi password typed on the portal is not readable by someone in radio range who does not know it.
  * **Settings:** NVS is encrypted (XTS-AES) with keys derived through the HMAC peripheral from a random key in eFuse block `KEY5`, which software cannot read back. A new board burns that key at its first start, and this cannot be undone. A partition found without the `encrypted` marker is erased once, because settings stored before encryption would otherwise stay on the flash in the clear. The Wi-Fi password is never returned or logged.
  * **Not covered:** without Secure Boot, someone holding the board can flash a program that asks the chip to decrypt NVS. Flash encryption and Secure Boot would close this, but they restrict flashing over USB.
* **Updates:** two app slots of 1856 KB each. OTA cannot change the partition table, so the pack's two slots of 96 KB have room for 512 effects of today's size, twice the 256 a pack may hold now (`kMaxPackEffects` in `src/effect_catalog.h`), and a later version that needed more room would need a flash over USB. Install from the portal, or with `pwsh -File deploy\ota-update.ps1 -Address csigarland.local`.
  * **Update file:** format 3 (`src/ota_logic.h`) is a header, the signing certificate, the effects pack, then `firmware.bin`. The header gives each part's size and SHA-256 and ends in an ECDSA P-256 signature over the rest of itself. A file may leave out the pack or the image, and the board installs what it holds and keeps the rest: every build makes `firmware.ota` with both, `firmware-only.ota` and `effects.ota` in the environment's folder under `.pio\build`. The portal's Firmware page and `ota-update.ps1` read the header and show what a file holds before sending it. Pairing decides who may send one, and the signature what may run: a paired browser still cannot install code you did not sign. The board checks the certificate and the signature before touching any flash, and each part's digest as it arrives; only when every part has passed, and the image's own checks too, does the new pack take effect and the boot slot change. A board running a firmware that reads an earlier format, or with an earlier partition table, needs one flash over USB to take this one.
  * **Certificates:** the firmware holds a private root, `csiGarland Root CA`, the owner's. It issues the signing certificate, valid for code signing only, which each update carries, so a new signing key needs no new firmware. `deploy/new-signing-certificate.ps1` makes both in `cert/` at the repository root, the root key under a passphrase. Git ignores that folder. Its `-RenewSigning` replaces the signing certificate only; `-Force` replaces the root too, and boards trusting the old root then need a flash over USB. A build without these files stops, unless `CSIGARLAND_THROWAWAY_SIGNING` makes a chain nobody keeps, as CI does. The board does not check validity dates, as it may have no clock. A certificate cannot be revoked; a leaked signing key is answered by replacing the root in new firmware over USB.
  * **Downgrades:** a signed older version installs too, so an update can be undone, and so a paired browser, or anyone who has joined the setup network, can downgrade to a version you signed.
  * **A closed unit:** with no USB, a failed trial would otherwise be seen by nobody. While on trial the firmware keeps its log and hands it out at `GET /trial-log` over plain HTTP, open and read-only, with a first line `version=... on_trial=... https=... boot=... image=... pack=...`, and `ota-update.ps1` prints it as it comes and saves it in `temp/`. Plain HTTP starts before HTTPS, and on trial waits for the network and 3 s more first, so the log gets out before anything risky runs. If HTTPS cannot start, by a failure rather than a crash, the portal stays up on plain HTTP, with a non-`Secure` cookie of another name (`csigh`, as a browser will not let plain HTTP replace a `Secure` `csig`) and a warning on the Info page, and `/trial-log` keeps answering: the firmware still confirms itself, as it can take the next update. On plain HTTP the cookie and everything else travel unencrypted, so install a fix soon, and do not forward a port meanwhile.
  * **Trial:** a new image is confirmed after 60 s if its settings opened, either server started, its Lighting and schedule tasks exist, and its buttons and screen were set up. Those last two go by what the drivers report, and a panel that is simply not there reports nothing. If it restarts for any reason before that, a power cut included, the bootloader returns to the previous image; a new try is then needed. It counts as confirmed only once the flash, read back, has this image valid and booting: if that fails five times it restarts and goes back, and `on_trial` stays true until then. A record that cannot be read also counts as on trial.
  * **During an update** the screen shows progress and the screensaver stays off. The update asks the lighting task to stop touching the LED output, and starts writing once it has answered, a frame in flight or a probe having finished, or after 1.5 s regardless, so that a lighting task gone wrong cannot keep a closed unit from its next firmware. RMT's interrupt runs through the writes, so a frame in flight still comes out whole. The LEDs hold their last frame, and on or off ordered meanwhile takes effect afterwards. A failed or refused update leaves the running firmware untouched, and the lighting task carries on. The web servers run below the screen's priority, so that decrypting an upload leaves it time to draw. Once it is installed, the restart waits up to 3 s more for the scheduler to save what was chosen meanwhile, as an Off of the countdown, and its answer counts only while it is of what is chosen at that moment, a choice of the same value again since it among what is not, so a change after it is saved too; a save that fails or does not come in time is logged, and what was installed stands. An update and a restart take turns: one asked while the other goes on is refused, and a restart reserved, as the renewal does before it prepares, is given back only if nothing asked for one meanwhile, so a renewal of the certificate waits for an update being received, and is looked at again at the next answer of NTP. The upload is refused at once if its length is more than the slots could take, and has 15 s for its header and certificate, then a minute and as long again as the file takes at 8 KB/s.
  * **Confirmation:** the page and `ota-update.ps1` report success only when `boot`, random at each start, has changed, `image` (the ELF hash in the image's descriptor, which they read from the file) and `pack_digest` are what the file holds, and `on_trial` is false. A version alone would not do, as one can repeat between builds. Another image means the garland went back. The page waits up to 150 s, the script 180 s.
  * **Firmware and pack together:** the pack is committed, then the boot slot switched. These are two flash writes, so they are not one atomic step: if the switch fails the pack is taken back, and only a power cut between them leaves the old firmware with the new pack, which refuses the effects it cannot play.
  * **Packs and firmware:** a pack in a file with firmware is not read by the running firmware, only checked against its digest, as it is for the firmware coming. Should a new firmware fail its trial, the old one finds the pack unreadable if its format is newer, and takes the other slot's. A pack with effects this firmware cannot play loses only those.
  * **API:** `GET /api/ota` `{"version", "on_trial", "trial_left", "effects", "pack_digest", "image", "boot"}`, where `trial_left` is the whole seconds of the trial left, counted by the firmware from the trial's start, all of it before the trial starts and 0 off trial or once its time is out; `POST /api/ota` with the update file as the body.
* **Remembered settings:** the effect, brightness and speed survive a restart, and so does whether the garland was playing or in standby, in the byte 1.0.0 kept as 0 or 1, which so reads as it did. Whether the playlist was is kept with the list itself, in one record under `playlist_rec`: the list's text, and when it was left playing, the values of `remembered` it was left playing with: up to two: while it plays, or as it starts, the record is written first, marked for the value about to be written and, only if the list played with it, the one there; as it stops, `remembered` is written first. Whichever a cut between the two writes leaves then reads as the state before or the state after, never a mix. A list is read at a start apart from its marks, and so is whether it played, and so are `remembered` and the schedule, each tried again if the reading fails, as a list left out would be out of reach until it is saved again, and a reading that failed is no "not playing". A stop first leaves only the mark of the value there, as an older one could match the value about to be written, and a value written again the same bytes is not written at all. One blob, written whole or not at all, so a list emptied, replaced or refused takes its mark with it, and no failure or power cut can part the two. The mark counts only while `remembered` is still those bytes, which 1.0.0 may change, and for a list that names an effect; the text alone, as builds before the record kept it under `playlist`, is still read, as a list not left playing. The scheduler and the server each read what the other writes before they write theirs, so the two are saved under one lock. Starting or stopping the playlist is saved at once. The rest are saved under one NVS key once all have stayed unchanged for 10 s, which keeps writes rare; turning on or off wakes the saving task at once, as the power often goes right after it, so a power cut within a moment of that can still bring back the old state. An LED output that cannot start refuses the power without changing what is remembered, so it plays again at a later start. A garland found missing does not: detection only advises. NVS itself spreads them over its pages and keeps the old value until a new one is complete. The effect is kept by id; one gone from the catalog falls back to the first favourite. One the catalog lacks while uploaded effects are not all read may be one of them: the first favourite plays for it meanwhile, and saves of the power, brightness or speed alone write its id, not the favourite's, until it is read and put on, the power and levels left as they are then, or the owner or a step chooses what plays, or all are read without it, which makes it one gone (`PendingEffect`, `effect_store_all_read`, which other tasks than the server's read as one atomic word). The schedule's replay at the first time sync can still replace them.

### Garland detection

WS2812B has no read-back and the board has no rail current sensor, so an exact count needs a hardware change: wiring the last LED's `DOUT` back to a GPIO. The firmware reads the data pin through ADC1:

* **Presence:** an open pin rises through the internal pull-up, measured at 2.66 V on this board. An LED on an unpowered rail holds the pin near one diode drop through its input protection diode, and one on a live rail held it at about 1.9 V. A pin below 2.3 V counts as present. This reading takes under a millisecond and works with the rail on or off, so it is repeated every 3 s, and a garland plugged in or removed at any time shows on the screen. The LEDs hold their frame meanwhile.
* **Count:** the same pull-up charges the rail capacitance, so the rise rate gives an estimate shown as `~N`. It runs before each power-on, as it needs a rail that is off and one decoupling capacitor per LED board, and it depends on the pull-up and capacitor tolerances; `kProbePullupKohm` and `kProbeCapPerLedNf` in `garland_probe_math.h` are the calibration points, and the probe logs its raw readings. It has not yet been run on a MOSFET-gated garland.

The LED count in use is the number set in the portal, otherwise the estimate while the probe reports a garland present and the estimate is above zero, otherwise `CSIGARLAND_LED_COUNT` (`effective_led_count` in `garland_state.h`). Detection is advisory: a garland reported as absent still gets power and data, at that fallback count.

An LED strip wired directly to 5 V, with no MOSFET, measured a steady 1.94 V. It reads as present with an unknown count (`LEDs: ?`), because a live rail has nothing to charge. No firmware change can count LEDs in that wiring.

## HTTPS Certificates

Each board is its own small certificate authority. Nothing about this is in the firmware image or an update file, so none of it can leak by publishing one, and no two boards share anything.

| | Root | Portal certificate |
|---|---|---|
| Key | P-256, made on the board | P-256, made on the board |
| Issued by | Itself | The root |
| Names | `CN=csiGarland root` | `<name>.local`, the domain if any, `192.168.4.1` |
| Lasts | 20 years | 825 days, as Apple's devices refuse longer |
| Installed by the owner | Yes, once per device | No |
| Made anew when | The domain changes, or within 60 days of its end | The mDNS name or domain changes, or within 60 days of its end, or the root is new |

**Keys are made only when missing.** Certificates are made on the keys there are, so a new domain needs no new key. A portal certificate saved is taken only if it reads, and its key reads and is the one it names: one that cannot serve, which the server would take and fail at every handshake, HTTPS up in name and the home network sent to it, is made again, and plain HTTP stays until one serves. A key is replaced only if certificates cannot be made with the saved one and reading it back shows it can never serve, as when it is damaged, and that takes the root's trust with it. A portal key that can never serve, under a root key that can, is made again with its certificate under that root, which browsers trust already. A renewal is weighed only once the keys and the domain were all read, and only once this start's making of certificates is over, made or failed, or the check of the saved one ran out of memory, which keeps it and serves plain HTTP this start, so a time sync while the maker runs weighs nothing, and not again for 6 h after a start whose making failed, as a restart into the same failure would only cut short the time HTTP has to take an update: names worked out from no domain would find any certificate stale, and restart for it at each time sync, while HTTP is all there is to update by. Running out of memory while reading a key says nothing of it, and nor does mbedTLS's bare invalid-format code, which it gives whatever stopped the import, out of key slots among it: a key whose bytes are bad comes back with an ASN.1 or PEM code beside it. Each key is read three times before it is judged, and one that is unknown is kept, with HTTP serving until a later start. The identity and the owner's domain are each read up to three times; one that still cannot be read is no setting missing, and that start has no HTTPS rather than a root made for the wrong domain. All of it lives in one blob in encrypted NVS, the domain apart. The check runs at each start, and the screen says `Making the HTTPS key` while there is work to do.

**Name constraints.** The root carries a critical NameConstraints extension: only `.local` names, the owner's one domain or fixed IPv4 address, and `192.168.4.1/32`. Installing it trusts it for those alone. A new domain or address therefore means a new root certificate on the same key, to be installed again; a new mDNS name does not, as it stays under `.local`. mbedTLS has no parser for this extension and refuses a certificate where it is critical, which matters only if the board reads its root back: it does not, and takes the fingerprint from the PEM's bytes. Browsers do enforce it. `tls_name_constraints_der` builds the value, and a test holds it to bytes checked against the `cryptography` package.

**Dates with no clock.** A board sets `notBefore` a day before the best date it knows: the clock if a module has set it by the time this runs, otherwise the latest of the sources' date (`CSIGARLAND_SOURCE_DATE` in `platformio.ini`, which the build gives the compiler in place of the day it runs) and the last date kept for this. The network manager runs while the startup waits for the certificates to be made, so NTP may answer before they are or after, and the portal goes by the time it read when it began; so when an answer shows a certificate within 60 days of its end, the scheduler keeps that date and restarts the garland, and the certificate is renewed at that start. A clock module that ran ahead can leave a certificate that starts in the future. An NTP answer that shows this does the same, and only such an answer does, as a start with no clock has nothing but an older date to go by: only the certificate ruled out is made anew, so the root stays unless it was made under that clock too, and then browsers have to be given the new one. That finding stays saved until the new certificate is, so a power cut in between repeats the work at the next start, clock or no clock.

**The check at first contact.** A browser that has not installed the root cannot tell the garland from an impostor, and the download itself is unverified. The SHA-256 on the Network page comes over the same channel. The serial log's `root SHA-256` line is the one independent check, and it takes USB. `/trial-log` carries the same line on a closed unit, over the same network as the page, so there it proves nothing about who answers, and such a unit has no independent check at first contact: the root is to be installed on a network that can be trusted.

**Limits.** NVS encryption ties these keys to the chip's eFuse key, but without Secure Boot someone holding the board can flash a program that reads them, as with every setting.

## Tasks

The chip has one core, and FreeRTOS runs the highest-priority task that is ready.

| Task | Priority | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| `lighting` | 20 (`configMAX_PRIORITIES - 5`) | Rail gate, data pin, probe, RMT output. Reads state, draws, limits current, dithers |
| ESP-IDF's Wi-Fi and network tasks | Above the ones below | The radio and TCP/IP |
| `esp_timer` | 22 | Dither ticks, button callbacks, auth timer. Button callbacks change memory only |
| LVGL (`esp_lvgl_port`) | 4 | The screen. Draws `screen_model.h`'s model of the state |
| `httpd` for HTTPS and for HTTP | 3 | One mutex lets only one run a handler at a time, as handlers share static buffers |
| `scheduler` | 2 | Clock, schedule, saving remembered settings |
| `events` | 2 | The push streams: the state every 250 ms, sent when it changed, and a comment every 15 s |
| `net_manager`, `captive_dns` | 2 | Access point policy, reconnecting, scans, captive DNS |
| `tls_maker` | 2 | Exists only while keys or certificates are being made |

**State (`garland_state.h`)** is the only thing these share for control. Power, what the owner chose, effect, brightness and speed are one atomic word, so an action, as from the schedule, arrives whole and the lighting task never sees part of it. Other fields stand alone. No task can block the lighting task on a lock. Parsing is the one place it does more than draw: at a change of effect it parses that effect's JSON from the mapped pack, less than a frame's work with heap allocations, whose upper bound has not been measured.

**Effects in RAM.** The catalog keeps pointers into the mapped pack and parses only the effect that plays. Uploaded effects stay parsed, as they live in NVS. One whose file could not be read at the start is no effect missing, nor is the store empty whose namespace or list of files could not be had, or stopped short, or whose partition is there but could not be readied, as when memory runs short (a partition the table lacks is a store known empty): the partition is readied again, and the file, or the whole list, read again before each upload, and before the effects list is answered or a favourite changed, and while any is left, an upload under an id the garland does not know is answered 503, as that file may hold it, and would come back at the next start once the upload is deleted (`UnreadStoreKeys`). Favourites read meanwhile that name an effect the catalog lacks may name one of those: the list on the state lacks it for now, a change of a favourite is answered 503, as its save would drop that mark, and once all are read the list is taken again from flash, onto the catalog as it then is.

## Startup and Failure

In order, from `main.cpp`:

1. Blank the chain, as LEDs on an ungated rail keep their colours through a reset.
2. Open the effects pack; without a good one, the one effect in the firmware.
3. Settings. Failure to open them leaves them untouched, and the start goes on: the setup network's password is made all the same, and a new one at each start that cannot save it. Then the uploaded effects, from their own partition whatever came of the settings, and the LED count, favourites and screen settings, the playlist and the remembered choice, each read on its own and tried again if it cannot be (`src/looks.cpp`, `src/playlist_resolver.cpp`).
4. Lighting task, screen, access control, buttons. Buttons work whatever else fails.
5. Clock module and scheduler, then network and portal, whether or not the settings opened: a closed garland has no other way to its next update.

| If this fails | Then |
|---|---|
| A pack slot | The other slot, or the one built-in effect |
| Settings: pages full of nothing, or of a later format | Erased, as nothing else can be done with them |
| Settings: a missing encryption marker | Erased once, as unencrypted settings would otherwise stay on the flash. An erase takes NVS out of use first, so one that fails, or whose bringing up again fails, leaves none, and Wi-Fi starts without it, as below |
| Settings: anything else | Kept as they are and reported, nothing saved over the remembered choice, and their reads tried again. The garland plays on its buttons, in UTC, with no home network known, so its setup network comes up, the password on the screen, and its plain HTTP takes the next update; with no NVS at all Wi-Fi starts without it. Paired browsers that are there and cannot be read let none in, and are not saved over. The LED count, favourites and screen settings that cannot be read keep their defaults and are read again every 5 s; until they are, the effects list and the screen settings are answered 503, read and changed alike, as a page would save the defaults back and a backup keep them as the owner's, and the Device page keeps its screen form closed until it has what is saved; a change of the LED count, which saves only itself, is taken. Uploaded effects load from their own partition all the same. New firmware on trial goes back |
| HTTPS, or making certificates | The reason is logged, and the portal stays up on plain HTTP so that the next update can reach it. New firmware on trial still confirms itself. The Info page warns |
| Plain HTTP only | Logged. HTTPS and updates work; no redirect, and no captive portal on the setup network |
| Both servers | New firmware on trial goes back |
| Setting up a button or the screen | Logged. The rest works, and new firmware on trial goes back |
| Wi-Fi | Keeps trying; the setup network comes up after 20 s |
| A scan that never ends | Given up after 20 s, and stopped in the driver |

## What Is Saved When

| What | Where | When |
|---|---|---|
| On or off | NVS | The saving task is woken at once. A power cut in that moment can bring back the old state |
| Effect, brightness, speed | NVS | Once unchanged for 10 s |
| Paired browsers | NVS | A new one before its browser hears of it. Forgetting reports whether it was saved |
| Keys and certificates | NVS | When made. If saving fails they still serve, and the next start makes new ones |
| Effects pack | Pack slot | Written to the idle slot, its record last. A power cut leaves the old one in charge |

**Flash writes and the LEDs.** A flash write stops every task and holds back every interrupt not in IRAM, and a frame longer than RMT's memory needs its refills on time. So `CONFIG_RMT_TX_ISR_CACHE_SAFE` is on: RMT's interrupt and its encoders, `led_strip`'s included, live in IRAM and run through the write, and a frame in flight comes out whole, whoever is writing. No new frame starts until the write ends, so dithering stops for that moment and the LEDs hold their colours. Updates pause the lighting task as well, for their long run of writes.
