# csiGarland Software Architecture

What the firmware in `firmware/app` does now. Plans are in [`stubs/ROADMAP.md`](stubs/ROADMAP.md), and how to build and use it is in the [guides](guides/README.md).

## What It Does

* **Startup:** the first thing the application does, on a cold start and after any reset, is open the rail for a moment, send two black frames for 256 LEDs and close it again. LEDs on an ungated rail keep their last colours until then, through the bootloader. It then stays in standby with the rail off and checks for a garland.
* **POWER (GPIO 3):** a click in standby turns on at the remembered brightness (50 % at first); when on, a click steps brightness 10 → 25 → 50 → 75 → 100 → 10 %; a long press (0.8 s) starts a 5 s countdown on the screen, with the garland still lit, and then turns off.
* **EFFECT (GPIO 20):** when on, a click moves to the next favourite effect and a long press goes to the first favourite. See the [Effects Guide](guides/effects.md).
* **SPEED (GPIO 23):** when on, a click steps speed 0.25x → 0.5x → 0.75x → 1x → 1.5x → 2x → 3x → 0.25x and a long press returns to 1x. From a speed set on the portal between these, a click goes to the next step up, or to 0.25x from 3x or more.
* **Any button** cancels the countdown or wakes the screen from a screensaver. That press does nothing else.
* **Display:** LVGL screen in csiPass's design and palette: pixel wordmark (`csi` white, `Garland` blue), build line and rule, a framed body with a status icon, status word, effect name and one evenly spaced row of brightness, speed and LED count, each a glyph in the frame's colour and a white value (LED count: plain `N` when set manually, `~N` when estimated, `0` when none are detected, `?` when present but not measurable), and a framed footer with the portal address. The whole screen turns amber when no LEDs are detected. The image is rotated 180 degrees from the panel's default, with its top at the board's USB end.
* **Screensaver:** ported from csiPass: None, Starfield (default), Flying Clock (local time, or uptime before the first time sync), Breathing Dot, Black Screen (backlight off). It starts when no button has been pressed for the delay, 10-600 s with a default of 60. The portal sets both, and it never runs over the countdown, an update, an access request or the making of keys.
* **Screen brightness:** the backlight is dimmed by PWM. The portal sets its level for normal use (default 100 %) and during a screensaver (default 30 %), each 5-100 %, applied at once and saved. Black Screen always turns it off.
* **LED brightness:** the percent is perceptual (gamma 2.2), so 10, 25, 50 and 75 % send about 0.5, 5, 22 and 53 % of the light. Brightness and the limiter work in 1/256ths of an LED step, and temporal dithering carries that to the LEDs: frames go out every 2.5 ms, 400 a second, each channel alternating between the two steps around its level. A chain too long for that dithers as fast as it can be sent (about 120 frames a second at 256 LEDs). Effects are still drawn about 60 times a second.
* **Effects:** described as data, with a catalog of ready and planned (`todo`) effects. See the [Effects Guide](guides/effects.md). They ship as a pack beside the firmware, in two 96 KB flash slots written in turn (`src/effect_pack_store.cpp`), so a flash, an update file or an effects-only update can replace them. The owner can add up to 32 from files, kept in the 64 KB `effects` flash partition (`src/effect_store.cpp`).
* **Screens:** after csiPass, `src/screen_model.h` holds one row per screen: its colour, title, text, and whether a screensaver may cover it. One function chooses the screen from the state, in the order update, making keys, access request, power-off countdown, setup network, no LEDs, playing, standby; `display_view.cpp` only draws the result, and knows nothing of the panel, which `display.cpp` drives. So `tools/screens` can draw the same screens to files for the documents, and fails if a screen has no picture. A new screen or a change of colour is a change to `screen_model.h`.
* **Screen colours:** the accent colours the rule, frames, icon, title, stat glyphs and progress bar, and says one thing: grey, nothing is happening (standby); blue, running and all is well (playing); amber, the owner has something to do or must not interfere (no LEDs, setup network, power-off countdown, update under way, access request, making keys); green, an operation has finished well (update installed). Red is kept for a fault the board cannot get over, and no screen uses it yet. Whatever the state, text and the bottom line are white, the version and the clock module's glyph grey, and the wordmark its own colours.
* **Web portal:** six pages, and one for pairing a browser, sharing one stylesheet and script, each under a header with the firmware version: Effects (`/`) shows the current mode, plays any ready effect and picks favourites, Schedule (`/schedule`) holds the next action, the actions, the time zone and the clock module, Device (`/device`) the LED count (automatic or 1-256) and the screen, Network (`/network`) the home network, the mDNS name, the root certificate with how to install it, and the owner's domain, Firmware (`/firmware`) updates, Info (`/info`) the board, memory, addresses, mode, next action and paired browsers. Favourites, the LED count, the name, the domain and Wi-Fi settings are saved in NVS. On the setup network a captive portal opens the Network page. `scripts/portal_pack.py` gzips all of this at build time, to about a third of its size as the build prints it, and flash holds only that form. The stylesheet and script are served under names holding a hash of their content (`/s.<hash>.css`), which browsers may keep for good; the pages link to the current names and are checked with the garland on each load. Class names are one letter, and the pages carry no inline styles. A blue button is the one main action of its block, grey is any other, equals with no main one among them included, and red text marks one that loses something (Delete, Forget). In a list, the blue button is what is in force (Active).
* **Network:** with no home network configured, or after 20 s without it, the access point `csiGarland-Setup` (WPA2, with the board's own password on its screen) runs at `http://192.168.4.1`, with a captive portal that answers every DNS name with that address. On the home network the portal is at `https://` and the address shown on the display, or `https://csigarland.local` (mDNS, under a name the Network page can change, which restarts the garland, as the certificate holds the name), and an access point that was running stops 60 s after connecting. The station picks the strongest access point of the network's name, tries it 3 times before moving to the next, and runs without Wi-Fi power save, as the unit is mains powered.
* **Time:** the clock syncs with `pool.ntp.org` on every home network connection and hourly after that. Without a clock module, after a power cut it stays unset until the next sync. The first browser to open the portal sets the time zone from its own; the portal can change it.
* **Clock module (optional):** a DS3231 on the `TX` and `RX` pads, wiring in [`hardware/control-unit.md`](hardware/control-unit.md). Looked for at startup; without one everything works as above. A module that has kept its time sets the clock at once, so the schedule works with no network, and it follows NTP hourly when it has drifted by 2 s or more. One that lost its time (the part's oscillator-stopped flag, or a date before 2026) is not believed: the first NTP answer sets it. The portal's Schedule section shows it and sets it from the browser's clock, from NTP or by hand, and the next NTP answer overrules a time set there. Since these pads are UART0, logs go over USB.
* **Schedule:** up to 32 actions, each with a time, days of the week and steps: turn on or off, brightness 1-100 %, speed (0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2 or 3), effect. Nothing can accompany "off", and starting an effect also turns the garland on. Actions run only once the clock is set. At the first sync after boot, only what came after the last saved choice is replayed as one result, up to 24 hours: the time of that choice is saved with it, so a schedule step from before it never overrides it. Choosing what is already playing counts too, and is saved like a change, as the moment is what matters. The same holds while running: a step already due when the owner chose something, in the same minute too, is not carried out, and the next one is. A mark of the owner's having chosen shares one atomic word with what it guards, cleared before a step is worked out, so a step worked out before a choice is never applied after it. A choice within a few milliseconds of a minute's start cannot be told from one just after it, and counts as in that minute. A choice saved with no clock outranks every step up to the first time known, and is saved again as of that time, so that later steps count, with another try each minute if that write fails. Actions go by local time: when the clocks go back, one in the repeated hour runs twice, and when they go forward, one in the skipped hour does not run that day.
* **Portal API:** `src/portal.cpp` holds the routes and the two servers, `src/portal_effects.cpp` the garland's state, LEDs and effects, and `src/portal_settings.cpp` Wi-Fi, the schedule, the time and the screen. `GET /api/display`, `POST /api/display` `{"saver", "delay", "screen_brightness", "saver_brightness"}` (`saver`: `none`, `starfield`, `flying-clock`, `breathing-dot`, `black-screen`), `GET /api/schedule`, `POST /api/schedule` `{"entries", "revision"}` (replaces the whole list, and answers 409 if `revision` is given and is not the list's own, which the page always does; both also give `next`, what is due next, and `revision`), `GET /api/info`, `POST /api/network/name` `{"name"}`, `POST /api/network/certificate` `{"domain"}` (empty for none; restarts), `GET /csigarland-root.crt`, `POST /api/auth/request` `{"label"}`, `POST /api/auth/wait` `{"request"}`, `GET /api/auth/devices`, `POST /api/auth/forget` `{"id"}`, `GET /api/time`, `POST /api/time` `{"zone"}` (POSIX TZ string), `POST /api/time/rtc` `{"source": "ntp"}`, `{"source": "browser", "epoch"}` (a whole number within 2026-2099) or `{"source": "manual", "local": "2026-10-31T18:05"}`, `GET /api/state`, `POST /api/state` `{"brightness", "speed"}` (either or both, `speed` 0.25 to 5 in steps of 0.25, and it turns nothing on), `GET /api/effects`, `POST` and `DELETE /api/effects/file?id=`, `POST /api/effects/preview`, `GET /api/effects/store`, `GET /api/effects/planned`, `POST /api/effect` `{"id"}`, `POST /api/favorites` `{"id", "favorite"}`, `POST /api/leds` `{"count"}` (0 means automatic), `GET /api/wifi`, `POST /api/wifi` `{"ssid", "password"}` (an empty `ssid` forgets the network), `POST /api/wifi/scan` starts a background search and `GET /api/wifi/scan` reports `{"state", "networks"}`, with `state` one of `idle`, `running`, `done`, `failed`.
* **Security:** the portal runs over HTTPS on port 443, and every `/api/` route but the two that ask for access wants a paired browser, whose cookie counts only from the portal's own origin: `SameSite` alone would let a page on another name under the owner's domain use it. The [public API](stubs/PUBLIC-API.md) under `/api/v1/` holds to the same rule with a token instead of a cookie, and its open routes are its two that ask for access and `/api/v1/about`, which says only what firmware this is. The firmware's log is open to nobody unpaired, and is kept in RAM only once a client has read it. Plain HTTP on port 80 only redirects there, except on the setup network, for `/trial-log` while on trial, and if HTTPS has failed (see **A closed unit** below).
  * **Pairing (`src/portal_auth.cpp`):** a browser asks, and it and the garland's screen show the same four digits. The owner presses POWER and SPEED together on seeing them match; any one button refuses and holds off new requests for a minute. One request waits at a time, for 60 s. The digits are what stop a stranger who asks at the moment the owner presses for their own browser. The browser receives a random 256-bit token as an `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Strict` cookie for 100 days, not extended by use, and the board keeps its SHA-256 in encrypted NVS, for up to 8 browsers. A token counts only once that is saved. Forgetting takes effect in memory at once, and says so if it could not be saved, as a restart could then bring the browser back. Without a clock the board cannot call a token expired, and starts its days at the first time it knows. The Info page lists the browsers and forgets them one by one; POWER and SPEED held for 10 s forget them all. `deploy/ota-update.ps1` pairs the same way and keeps its token in `deploy/portal-token.local.txt`. An accepted limit: the digits stop a second asker, not someone relaying. Whoever intercepts plain HTTP before its redirect to HTTPS can ask in the owner's place and show the owner the same digits, and an approval then gives them the token, as it is not tied to the owner's connection. What stops that is opening `https://` with the root installed. HSTS would do that for the browser, and is not sent on purpose: it would keep the browser from the plain HTTP that a firmware whose HTTPS has failed depends on.
  * **Certificates (`src/portal_tls.cpp`, `src/tls_logic.h`):** see [HTTPS certificates](#https-certificates).
  * **Setup network exception:** requests to `192.168.4.1` need neither TLS nor pairing, as the phone windows that open it refuse a certificate they do not know and keep no cookies. Joining that network took the password on the screen. As no cookie stands in the way, the API, the two routes that ask for access and the other open ones among it, answers only to the portal's own names, `192.168.4.1` and `<name>.local`, and refuses a request whose `Origin` differs from the name it was sent to. So another page open on that phone cannot send to the portal, even under a name that network's DNS resolves to it. A page asked for under any other name is redirected to the portal's address. A request with no `Origin` is let through, as tools send none and joining took the password.
  * **Memory:** TLS uses dynamic buffers (16 KB in, 4 KB out, only while a record is in flight), P-256 only and no session cache or renegotiation, at most 4 sessions. Measured on a board during development: 106 KB lowest free heap with TLS working, under light use. An upload over TLS with other pages open has not been measured.
  * **Setup network:** each board makes its own random 12-character password once and shows it on the screen only while the access point runs, so the Wi-Fi password typed on the portal is not readable by someone in radio range who does not know it.
  * **Settings:** NVS is encrypted (XTS-AES) with keys derived through the HMAC peripheral from a random key in eFuse block `KEY5`, which software cannot read back. A new board burns that key at its first start, and this cannot be undone. A partition found without the `encrypted` marker is erased once, because settings stored before encryption would otherwise stay on the flash in the clear. The Wi-Fi password is never returned or logged.
  * **Not covered:** without Secure Boot, someone holding the board can flash a program that asks the chip to decrypt NVS. Flash encryption and Secure Boot would close this, but they restrict flashing over USB.
* **Updates:** two app slots of 1856 KB each. OTA cannot change the partition table, so the pack's two slots of 96 KB hold room for 512 effects, and a later version that needed more would need a flash over USB. Install from the portal, or with `pwsh -File deploy\ota-update.ps1 -Address csigarland.local`.
  * **Update file:** format 3 (`src/ota_logic.h`) is a header, the signing certificate, the effects pack, then `firmware.bin`. The header gives each part's size and SHA-256 and ends in an ECDSA P-256 signature over the rest of itself. A file may leave out the pack or the image, and the board installs what it holds and keeps the rest: every build makes `firmware.ota` with both, `firmware-only.ota` and `effects.ota` in `.pio\build\esp32c6`. The portal's Firmware page and `ota-update.ps1` read the header and show what a file holds before sending it. Pairing decides who may send one, and the signature what may run: a paired browser still cannot install code you did not sign. The board checks the certificate and the signature before touching any flash, and each part's digest as it arrives; only when every part has passed, and the image's own checks too, does the new pack take effect and the boot slot change. A board running a firmware that reads an earlier format, or with an earlier partition table, needs one flash over USB to take this one.
  * **Certificates:** the firmware holds a private root, `csiGarland Root CA`, the owner's. It issues the signing certificate, valid for code signing only, which each update carries, so a new signing key needs no new firmware. `deploy/new-signing-certificate.ps1` makes both in `cert/` at the repository root, the root key under a passphrase. Git ignores that folder. Its `-RenewSigning` replaces the signing certificate only; `-Force` replaces the root too, and boards trusting the old root then need a flash over USB. A build without these files stops, unless `CSIGARLAND_THROWAWAY_SIGNING` makes a chain nobody keeps, as CI does. The board does not check validity dates, as it may have no clock. A certificate cannot be revoked; a leaked signing key is answered by replacing the root in new firmware over USB.
  * **Downgrades:** a signed older version installs too, so an update can be undone, and so a paired browser, or anyone who has joined the setup network, can downgrade to a version you signed.
  * **A closed unit:** with no USB, a failed trial would otherwise be seen by nobody. While on trial the firmware keeps its log and hands it out at `GET /trial-log` over plain HTTP, open and read-only, with a first line `version=... on_trial=... https=... boot=... image=... pack=...`, and `ota-update.ps1` prints it as it comes and saves it in `temp/`. Plain HTTP starts before HTTPS, and on trial waits for the network and 3 s more first, so the log gets out before anything risky runs. If HTTPS cannot start, by a failure rather than a crash, the portal stays up on plain HTTP, with a non-`Secure` cookie of another name (`csigh`, as a browser will not let plain HTTP replace a `Secure` `csig`) and a warning on the Info page, and `/trial-log` keeps answering: the firmware still confirms itself, as it can take the next update. On plain HTTP the cookie and everything else travel unencrypted, so install a fix soon, and do not forward a port meanwhile.
  * **Trial:** a new image is confirmed after 60 s if either server started, its Lighting and schedule tasks exist, and its buttons and screen were set up. Those last two go by what the drivers report, and a panel that is simply not there reports nothing. If it restarts for any reason before that, a power cut included, the bootloader returns to the previous image; a new try is then needed. It counts as confirmed only once the flash, read back, has this image valid and booting: if that fails five times it restarts and goes back, and `on_trial` stays true until then. A record that cannot be read also counts as on trial.
  * **During an update** the screen shows progress and the screensaver stays off. The update asks the lighting task to stop touching the LED output, and starts writing once it has answered, a frame in flight or a probe having finished, or after 1.5 s regardless, so that a lighting task gone wrong cannot keep a closed unit from its next firmware. RMT's interrupt runs through the writes, so a frame in flight still comes out whole. The LEDs hold their last frame, and on or off ordered meanwhile takes effect afterwards. A failed or refused update leaves the running firmware untouched, and the lighting task carries on. The web servers run below the screen's priority, so that decrypting an upload leaves it time to draw.
  * **Confirmation:** the page and `ota-update.ps1` report success only when `boot`, random at each start, has changed, `image` (the ELF hash in the image's descriptor, which they read from the file) and `pack_digest` are what the file holds, and `on_trial` is false. A version alone would not do, as one can repeat between builds. Another image means the garland went back. The page waits up to 150 s, the script 180 s.
  * **Firmware and pack together:** the pack is committed, then the boot slot switched. These are two flash writes, so they are not one atomic step: if the switch fails the pack is taken back, and only a power cut between them leaves the old firmware with the new pack, which refuses the effects it cannot play.
  * **Packs and firmware:** a pack in a file with firmware is not read by the running firmware, only checked against its digest, as it is for the firmware coming. Should a new firmware fail its trial, the old one finds the pack unreadable if its format is newer, and takes the other slot's. A pack with effects this firmware cannot play loses only those.
  * **API:** `GET /api/ota` `{"version", "on_trial", "trial_left", "effects", "pack_digest", "image", "boot"}`, where `trial_left` is the whole seconds of the trial left, counted by the firmware from the trial's start, all of it before the trial starts and 0 off trial or once its time is out; `POST /api/ota` with the update file as the body.
* **Remembered settings:** the effect, brightness and speed survive a restart, and so does whether the garland was playing or in standby. They are saved under one NVS key once all have stayed unchanged for 10 s, which keeps writes rare; turning on or off wakes the saving task at once, as the power often goes right after it, so a power cut within a moment of that can still bring back the old state. An LED output that cannot start refuses the power without changing what is remembered, so it plays again at a later start. A garland found missing does not: detection only advises. NVS itself spreads them over its pages and keeps the old value until a new one is complete. The effect is kept by id; one gone from the catalog falls back to the first favourite. The schedule's replay at the first time sync can still replace them.

### Garland detection

WS2812B has no read-back and the board has no rail current sensor, so an exact count needs a hardware change: wiring the last LED's `DOUT` back to a GPIO. The firmware reads the data pin through ADC1:

* **Presence:** an open pin rises through the internal pull-up, measured at 2.66 V on this board. An LED on an unpowered rail holds the pin near one diode drop through its input protection diode, and one on a live rail held it at about 1.9 V. A pin below 2.3 V counts as present. This reading takes under a millisecond and works with the rail on or off, so it is repeated every 3 s, and a garland plugged in or removed at any time shows on the screen. The LEDs hold their frame meanwhile.
* **Count:** the same pull-up charges the rail capacitance, so the rise rate gives an estimate shown as `~N`. It runs before each power-on, as it needs a rail that is off and one decoupling capacitor per LED board, and it depends on the pull-up and capacitor tolerances; `kProbePullupKohm` and `kProbeCapPerLedNf` in `garland_probe_math.h` are the calibration points, and the probe logs its raw readings. It has not yet been run on a MOSFET-gated garland.

The LED count in use is the number set in the portal, otherwise the estimate while the probe reports a garland present and the estimate is above zero, otherwise `CSIGARLAND_LED_COUNT` (`effective_led_count` in `garland_state.h`). Detection is advisory: a garland reported as absent still gets power and data, at that fallback count.

An LED strip wired directly to 5 V, with no MOSFET, measured a steady 1.94 V. It reads as present with an unknown count (`LEDs: ?`), because a live rail has nothing to charge. No firmware change can count LEDs in that wiring.

## HTTPS Certificates

Each board is its own small certificate authority. Nothing about this is in the firmware image or an update file, so none of it can leak by publishing one, and no two boards share anything.

| | Root | Portal certificate |
|---|---|---|
| Key | P-256, made on the board | P-256, made on the board |
| Issued by | Itself | The root |
| Names | `CN=csiGarland root` | `<name>.local`, the domain if any, `192.168.4.1` |
| Lasts | 20 years | 825 days, as Apple's devices refuse longer |
| Installed by the owner | Yes, once per device | No |
| Made anew when | The domain changes, or within 60 days of its end | The mDNS name or domain changes, or within 60 days of its end, or the root is new |

**Keys are made only when missing.** Certificates are made on the keys there are, so a new domain needs no new key. A key is replaced only if certificates cannot be made with the saved one, as when it is damaged, and that takes the root's trust with it. All of it lives in one blob in encrypted NVS. The check runs at each start, and the screen says `Making the HTTPS key` while there is work to do.

**Name constraints.** The root carries a critical NameConstraints extension: only `.local` names, the owner's one domain or fixed IPv4 address, and `192.168.4.1/32`. Installing it trusts it for those alone. A new domain or address therefore means a new root certificate on the same key, to be installed again; a new mDNS name does not, as it stays under `.local`. mbedTLS has no parser for this extension and refuses a certificate where it is critical, which matters only if the board reads its root back: it does not, and takes the fingerprint from the PEM's bytes. Browsers do enforce it. `tls_name_constraints_der` builds the value, and a test holds it to bytes checked against the `cryptography` package.

**Dates with no clock.** A board sets `notBefore` a day before the best date it knows: the clock if a module has set it by the time this runs, otherwise the latest of the sources' date (`CSIGARLAND_SOURCE_DATE` in `platformio.ini`, which the build gives the compiler in place of the day it runs) and the last date kept for this. The network manager runs while the startup waits for the certificates to be made, so NTP may answer before they are or after, and the portal goes by the time it read when it began; so when an answer shows a certificate within 60 days of its end, the scheduler keeps that date and restarts the garland, and the certificate is renewed at that start. A clock module that ran ahead can leave a certificate that starts in the future. An NTP answer that shows this does the same, and only such an answer does, as a start with no clock has nothing but an older date to go by: only the certificate ruled out is made anew, so the root stays unless it was made under that clock too, and then browsers have to be given the new one. That finding stays saved until the new certificate is, so a power cut in between repeats the work at the next start, clock or no clock.

**The check at first contact.** A browser that has not installed the root cannot tell the garland from an impostor, and the download itself is unverified. The SHA-256 on the Network page comes over the same channel. The serial log's `root SHA-256` line is the one independent check, and it takes USB. `/trial-log` carries the same line on a closed unit, over the same network as the page, so there it proves nothing about who answers, and such a unit has no independent check at first contact: the root is to be installed on a network that can be trusted.

**Limits.** NVS encryption ties these keys to the chip's eFuse key, but without Secure Boot someone holding the board can flash a program that reads them, as with every setting.

## Tasks

The chip has one core, and FreeRTOS runs the highest-priority task that is ready.

| Task | Priority | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| `lighting` | 20 (`configMAX_PRIORITIES - 5`) | Rail gate, data pin, probe, RMT output. Reads state, draws, limits current, dithers |
| ESP-IDF's Wi-Fi and network tasks | Above the ones below | The radio and TCP/IP |
| `esp_timer` | 22 | Dither ticks, button callbacks, auth timer. Button callbacks change memory only |
| LVGL (`esp_lvgl_port`) | 4 | The screen. Draws `screen_model.h`'s model of the state |
| `httpd` for HTTPS and for HTTP | 3 | One mutex lets only one run a handler at a time, as handlers share static buffers |
| `scheduler` | 2 | Clock, schedule, saving remembered settings |
| `net_manager`, `captive_dns` | 2 | Access point policy, reconnecting, scans, captive DNS |
| `tls_maker` | 2 | Exists only while keys or certificates are being made |

**State (`garland_state.h`)** is the only thing these share for control. Power, what the owner chose, effect, brightness and speed are one atomic word, so an action, as from the schedule, arrives whole and the lighting task never sees part of it. Other fields stand alone. No task can block the lighting task on a lock. Parsing is the one place it does more than draw: at a change of effect it parses that effect's JSON from the mapped pack, less than a frame's work with heap allocations, whose upper bound has not been measured.

**Effects in RAM.** The catalog keeps pointers into the mapped pack and parses only the effect that plays. Uploaded effects stay parsed, as they live in NVS.

## Startup and Failure

In order, from `main.cpp`:

1. Blank the chain, as LEDs on an ungated rail keep their colours through a reset.
2. Open the effects pack; without a good one, the one effect in the firmware.
3. Settings. Failure to open them leaves them untouched and the garland working without a portal.
4. Lighting task, screen, access control, buttons. Buttons work whatever else fails.
5. Clock module and scheduler, then network and portal, if settings opened.

| If this fails | Then |
|---|---|
| A pack slot | The other slot, or the one built-in effect |
| Settings: pages full of nothing, or of a later format | Erased, as nothing else can be done with them |
| Settings: a missing encryption marker | Erased once, as unencrypted settings would otherwise stay on the flash |
| Settings: anything else | Kept, reported, no portal. The garland plays on its buttons |
| HTTPS, or making certificates | The reason is logged, and the portal stays up on plain HTTP so that the next update can reach it. New firmware on trial still confirms itself. The Info page warns |
| Plain HTTP only | Logged. HTTPS and updates work; no redirect, and no captive portal on the setup network |
| Both servers | New firmware on trial goes back |
| Setting up a button or the screen | Logged. The rest works, and new firmware on trial goes back |
| Wi-Fi | Keeps trying; the setup network comes up after 20 s |
| A scan that never ends | Given up after 20 s, and stopped in the driver |

## What Is Saved When

| What | Where | When |
|---|---|---|
| On or off | NVS | The saving task is woken at once. A power cut in that moment can bring back the old state |
| Effect, brightness, speed | NVS | Once unchanged for 10 s |
| Paired browsers | NVS | A new one before its browser hears of it. Forgetting reports whether it was saved |
| Keys and certificates | NVS | When made. If saving fails they still serve, and the next start makes new ones |
| Effects pack | Pack slot | Written to the idle slot, its record last. A power cut leaves the old one in charge |

**Flash writes and the LEDs.** A flash write stops every task and holds back every interrupt not in IRAM, and a frame longer than RMT's memory needs its refills on time. So `CONFIG_RMT_TX_ISR_CACHE_SAFE` is on: RMT's interrupt and its encoders, `led_strip`'s included, live in IRAM and run through the write, and a frame in flight comes out whole, whoever is writing. No new frame starts until the write ends, so dithering stops for that moment and the LEDs hold their colours. Updates pause the lighting task as well, for their long run of writes.
