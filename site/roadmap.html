Roadmap

From the first release to the tree

The firmware 1.1.1 and the utility 1.3.0, for Windows, Linux and macOS, are released. Both are seen working on the owner's garland, the utility on Windows and on macOS. What is left is the hardware: printing the figurines, building the full garland, and measuring it. The roadmap in the repository is the one kept up to date.

Released

1.3.0 Utility

Released 6 November 2026. The utility alone, 1.3.0.44 for Windows, 1.3.0.45 for Linux, and the first for macOS, 1.3.0.42 for Apple silicon and 1.3.0.43 for Intel: an app with its icon, the token in the keychain, the system's own panels for files. It works with the firmware 1.1.0 and 1.1.1 alike.

1.1.1 Firmware

Released 6 November 2026. The firmware alone, 1.1.1.114: the figurines' new order along the cable and their new colours, and settings a start cannot read read again later, never saved over. A garland updated from 1.1.0 keeps all it saved.

1.2.0 Utility

Released 5 November 2026. The utility alone, 1.2.0.38 for Windows and 1.2.0.39 for Linux, the first for Linux: the token in the Secret Service of GNOME Keyring or KWallet, the desktop's own dialog for files, and one window at a time. The firmware stays 1.1.0.113. The Linux file builds again to the byte on Ubuntu 24.04, or in Docker on Windows.

1.1.0 Firmware and utility

Released 4 November 2026. Firmware 1.1.0.113 and utility 1.1.0.37: playlists with cross-fades, fades on turning on and off and a morning rise, backup and restore, the schedule on a 24-hour timeline, push updates, current in Tune, and a check of the API after an update. A garland updated from 1.0.0 keeps all it saved.

1.0.0 Firmware and utility

Released 3 November 2026. Firmware 1.0.0.102 and utility 1.0.0.27: 154 effects, the schedule and clock, the portal over HTTPS with its own root, signed updates with a trial and a way back, the public API, and the utility for Windows. Both build again to the byte from their sources.

What 1.3.0 and 1.1.1 brought

Tested, and seen working on the owner's garland: the firmware 1.1.1, and the utility on Windows and on macOS with Apple silicon. The file for Intel Macs has not been run yet.

The utility on macOS

An app for Apple silicon and Intel, with its icon in the Dock: the pairing token in the login keychain, files chosen in the system's own panels, and one window at a time. How to open it the first time is under its downloads.

The figurines' new order

Tree, star, snowman, pinecone, lantern, bell, icicle and gingerbread along the cable, so that neither colours nor shapes bunch together, and the effects that light a figurine by name in its new filament's colour.

Settings never saved over

What a start cannot read, from the paired browsers to the favourites and the screen, is read again later rather than taken for none, and the portal and the utility are told to ask again meanwhile.

A press during a slow start

A button pressed while the garland is still starting outranks the schedule's steps missed before it, and a turn on or off made then is saved at once.

A cookie for its own scheme

A paired browser's cookie counts only from a page of the same scheme as the server it reaches, so whoever intercepts plain HTTP cannot use the one HTTPS gave.

Planned

A current limit for your supply

Done, for 1.4.0: the Device page takes your power supply, 500 to 4400 mA, what one LED draws at full white and dark, and a margin of 5 to 20 % of what the supply gives beyond the board, and works out the limit, at most about 3.9 A, and how many LEDs show full white undimmed. One LED measured 36 mA where the firmware counted 55, so the full garland on white is no longer dimmed for nothing. The defaults keep the 1400 mA limit.

Scheduler24

Done, for 1.4.0: a screensaver for a garland with the clock module that says what comes next and how soon, the next scheduled action within a day or the playlist's next effect, or what happened last and how long ago.

Tune on your chain

Done, for the utility 1.4.0: the preview and Tune draw as many LEDs as your garland has, and chart their current, the part past the limit in red.

An update that has to answer

Coming in 1.4.0: a new firmware keeps itself only once it has answered the portal or the utility, which ask while they wait, within 10 minutes, and goes back to the one before otherwise. Then the portal and the utility show the Info page.

More platforms

The utility on Android and iOS, on the same Go code and the same public API.
