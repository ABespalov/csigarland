Roadmap

From the first release to the tree

The firmware and the utility 1.4.1 are released, the utility for Windows and Linux, with macOS to follow. Its updates over HTTPS are seen on the owner's garland, as the releases before them were, the utility on Windows and on macOS. The full garland is built, 32 figurines on a soldered harness, and its current measured under load. What is left: a day-long run, temperatures in the closed box, and the Christmas tree itself. The roadmap in the repository is the one kept up to date.

Released

1.4.1 Firmware and utility

Released 23 November 2026. Firmware 1.4.1.126, and the utility 1.4.1.66 for Windows and 1.4.1.67 for Linux, macOS to follow: updates over HTTPS that get in on a garland that has run a while, the LED count set by hand, and a Lab build for measuring a garland. A garland updated from 1.4.0 keeps all it saved, but for an LED count left on automatic, which takes 32.

1.4.0 Firmware and utility

Released 9 November 2026. Firmware 1.4.0.120, and the utility 1.4.0.52 for Windows, 1.4.0.53 for Linux, and 1.4.0.54 for Apple silicon and 1.4.0.55 for Intel Macs: a current limit worked out from your power supply, the Scheduler24 screensaver, an update kept only once it has answered, Tune on your garland's own chain, and a start that waits for what it could not read. A garland updated from 1.1.1 keeps all it saved.

1.3.0 Utility

Released 6 November 2026. The utility alone, 1.3.0.44 for Windows, 1.3.0.45 for Linux, and the first for macOS, 1.3.0.42 for Apple silicon and 1.3.0.43 for Intel: an app with its icon, the token in the keychain, the system's own panels for files. It works with the firmware 1.1.0 and 1.1.1 alike.

1.1.1 Firmware

Released 6 November 2026. The firmware alone, 1.1.1.114: the figurines' new order along the cable and their new colours, and settings a start cannot read read again later, never saved over. A garland updated from 1.1.0 keeps all it saved.

1.2.0 Utility

Released 5 November 2026. The utility alone, 1.2.0.38 for Windows and 1.2.0.39 for Linux, the first for Linux: the token in the Secret Service of GNOME Keyring or KWallet, the desktop's own dialog for files, and one window at a time. The firmware stays 1.1.0.113. The Linux file builds again to the byte on Ubuntu 24.04, or in Docker on Windows.

1.1.0 Firmware and utility

Released 4 November 2026. Firmware 1.1.0.113 and utility 1.1.0.37: playlists with cross-fades, fades on turning on and off and a morning rise, backup and restore, the schedule on a 24-hour timeline, push updates, current in Tune, and a check of the API after an update. A garland updated from 1.0.0 keeps all it saved.

1.0.0 Firmware and utility

Released 3 November 2026. Firmware 1.0.0.102 and utility 1.0.0.27: 154 effects, the schedule and clock, the portal over HTTPS with its own root, signed updates with a trial and a way back, the public API, and the utility for Windows. Both build again to the byte from their sources.

Milestones

  • 1. Electrical proof of conceptThe Hi-Link supply under load in the closed box, and the MOSFET gate at boot. Done: the gate cuts the LEDs off in standby; the automatic LED count was dropped, as it could not count this garland.
  • 2. 3D models and printsDone: the enclosure, and all 32 figurines in translucent PETG, their light and fit seen on the garland.
  • 3. Firmware on the boardDone: implemented, tested on the host, and seen on the owner's garland: the screen, gestures and screensavers with no flicker; the portal from a phone; NTP and the schedule; the clock module; every effect on eight figurines, one of each kind; an update, a refused signature and a return to the previous firmware.
  • 4. The 32-figurine garlandDone: the cable harness soldered, the figurines in their order along it, a base and a cap on every LED, and the current and voltage measured under load: 1012 mA on full white, 3.99 V at the last LED.
  • 5. On the treeA 24-hour run with the temperatures in the closed box, the current in every mode, and the garland on the Christmas tree.

What 1.4.1 brought

Tested on the host, and the portal's pages in a browser against a stand-in for the garland; its updates over HTTPS seen on the owner's garland, lit and in standby.

Updates that get in

mbedTLS takes its memory from 33 KB of its own, a slot for the 16.7 KB record buffer an upload asks for at every record and a pool for the rest, where a heap cut up by Wi-Fi buffers left that buffer no room and every update over HTTPS failed a second in.

The LED count by hand

The automatic estimate is gone: on the owner's garland it could count nothing. Set the number on the Device page; the screen shows it, and the garland still says whether anything is on the data line.

A Lab build

A firmware for measuring a garland, never published: why the board started, the chip's and the box's temperatures, memory and the current asked, and mbedTLS's memory, kept through an update's restart.

A base mount in two parts

A cup and a plug, so the garland is soldered and tested on the bench and the mounts close round it after, and caps of even 1.5 mm shells that glow alike.

The outlet's 3 A

The power settings allow a limit of up to about 3.9 A, but the control unit's garland outlet, a JST-XH, takes 3 A: with it, keep the limit at 3000 mA or below, or fit a connector rated for more.
